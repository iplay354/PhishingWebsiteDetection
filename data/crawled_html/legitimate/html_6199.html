Josh Shapiro (Democratic Party) is the Governor of Pennsylvania. He assumed office on January 17, 2023. His current term ends on January 19, 2027.

Shapiro received a bachelor's degree from the University of Rochester and a J.D. from Georgetown University.[1] Before running for office, Shapiro was a senior foreign policy advisor to U.S. Sen. Robert Torricelli (D), senior legislative assistant to U.S. Rep Peter Deutsch (D), chief of staff to U.S. Rep. Joseph Hoeffel (D), and attorney for Ballard Spahr Andrews & Ingersoll LLP.[2]

Shapiro was elected to represent District 153 in the Pennsylvania House of Representatives in 2004, and served in the House from 2005-2011. In 2011, Shapiro won election to the Montgomery County Board of Commissioners and served on the commission from 2012-2017 as chairman, the first time in over a century that Democrats controlled the commission.[3]

Shapiro won the 2016 election for attorney general of Pennsylvania against former State Sen. John Rafferty 51.4%-48.6%.

As attorney general, Shapiro released the results of a grand jury report on the Catholic Church's cover up of sexual abuse of more than 1,000 children over 70 years. Shapiro described his decision to continue the investigation after inheriting it in 2016 as "the most purposeful thing, short of giving life to our four children, I’ve ever done in my life...I made it clear I wanted the investigation to continue. I put the full force of our office into this investigation.”[4][5] Shapiro also forced settlements with pharmaceutical distributors for their role in the opioid epidemic, restricted redlining practices by a major mortgage lender, and filed corruption charges against two Democratic state legislators.[6]

Shapiro won the 2022 election for governor of Pennsylvania against state Sen. Doug Mastriano (R) 56.5%-41.7%. Shapiro's campaign focused on his record as attorney general and his potential ability as governor to veto legislation the legislature's Republican majority passes. Shapiro's campaign website said that he would veto certain legislation related to abortion and absentee/mail-in voting.[7] Heading into the election, Pennsylvania had a divided government, with a Democratic governor and Republican majorities in both legislative chambers. Shapiro's win maintained a divided government.

As governor, Shapiro implemented automatic voter registration allowing Pennsylvanians to register to vote at the Department of Transportation, signed legislation expanding insurance coverage for breast cancer screenings, and renewed funding for universal free school breakfast.[8]

During the 2023 legislative session, Shapiro explained why he line-item vetoed a provision in the 2024 budget to fund a private school tuition voucher program the legislature debated that session.[9][10] Though he supported the program during his gubernatorial campaign, Shapiro said, "Knowing that the two chambers will not reach consensus at this time to enact PASS, and unwilling to hold up our entire budget process over this issue, I will line-item veto the full $100 million appropriation and it will not be part of this budget bill."[9]

In July 2024, several media outlets reported that Shapiro was one of the finalists to become the running mate of Democratic Presidential nominee Kamala Harris (D) before she selected Minnesota Gov. Tim Walz (D).[11]

Biography

Josh Shapiro was born in Kansas City, Missouri.[12] Shapiro earned a law degree from Georgetown University. His career experience includes working as a congressional staffer, as chief of staff to former U.S. Representative Joe Hoeffel, and as an attorney.[13][14]

Primary race background

Main article: Pennsylvania races we're watching, 2016


The closely-watched race for Pennsylvania Attorney General was open following incumbent Kathleen Kane's (D) resignation on August 17, 2016, after she was found guilty on charges of perjury and abuse of her office. Kane, the first Democrat to hold the office since 1979, won in 2012 by 15 points. Shapiro and Stephen Zappala came out on top in a January 2016 poll. Zappala edged out Shapiro by one point while 49 percent of those surveyed were undecided; Shapiro was just a few points shy of the two-thirds majority vote required to earn the endorsement of the Pennsylvania Democratic Party in March.[16]

Noteworthy events

Fire at governor's residence (2025)

On Sunday, April 13, 2025, firefighters responded to a fire at the Pennsylvania governor’s residence, where Gov. Josh Shapiro (D), his family, and guests he was hosting were staying. The fire damaged significant portions of the residence and forced the occupants to evacuate.[25]

Later that day, Cody Balmer turned himself in to police and was charged with aggravated arson, aggravated assault, attempted criminal homicide, burglary, recklessly endangering another person, terrorism, and loitering. According to the criminal complaint, Balmer jumped the fence, broke a window, and threw an incendiary device into the residence.[26] On October 14, 2025, Balmer entered a plea deal and pleaded guilty to the attempted murder of Shapiro and related charges. He was sentenced to 25 to 50 years in prison.[27]


Reported as possible 2024 Democratic vice presidential nominee

See also: Vice presidential candidates, 2024

Media reports discussed Shapiro as a possible 2024 Democratic vice presidential candidate.[28] Vice President Kamala Harris (D) selected Minnesota Governor Tim Walz (D) as her running mate on August 6, 2024.[29]

In 2020, President Joe Biden (D) announced Vice President Kamala Harris (D) as his running mate six days before the start of the Democratic National Convention (DNC). In 2016, both Hillary Clinton (D) and Trump announced their running mates three days before the DNC and RNC, respectively.
