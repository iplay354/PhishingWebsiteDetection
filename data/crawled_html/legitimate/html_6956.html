Common questions about responsible gambling

Responsible gambling is about having fun with games of chance while making sure it doesn’t have a negative impact on your life or the lives of others. It’s enjoying a flutter now and then without letting it become an obsession or addiction. At the end of the day, gambling should remain entertainment rather than something that causes you stress or financial issues.

Set a budget for your leisure money and don’t spend more than you can reasonably afford to lose. Take breaks from gambling regularly so you don’t get caught up in the thrill of it. Stop if you start feeling out of control or like you need to chase losses. Support is available if you feel gambling may be affecting your wellbeing or relationships.

Lying about how much money or time you’ve spent gambling, chasing losses, gambling to escape problems or feelings of helplessness, losing interest in other activities or hobbies…these can all be signs it’s time to reevaluate your habits. Your mental health and relationships should come before any game. Know when to walk away.

Many areas have local support groups and helplines to call anonymously. Websites like GamblersAnonymous.org and NCPGambling.org connect those struggling to accredited treatment programs and counseling. Remember, just like any health issue, seeking help takes courage – your well-being is more important than any bets placed. You’ve got this.
