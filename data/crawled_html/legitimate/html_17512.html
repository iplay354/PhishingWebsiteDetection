Ichabods get started at UNLV

Ken Corbitt
Oct. 31, 2011, 2:03 p.m. CT

The first game of a season is always a test under fire, and Washburn’s basketball team expects to endure a lot of heat.

The Ichabods get their first taste of outside competition with an exhibition game at UNLV at 9 p.m. Tuesday at the Thomas & Mack Center in Las Vegas.

“You can find out a lot about yourself through these two games,” said WU coach Bob Chipman, who takes his team to Oklahoma on Sunday. “You give guys an opportunity to win positions, and the early Division I exhibitions help us with that process.

“We’re looking forward to the challenge and learning a lot about our team.”

Sophomore guard Jeff Reid is Washburn’s top returning scorer, averaging 9.9 points last season. Junior guard Will McNeill is back after missing last season with a broken foot, but two years ago he averaged 12.7 points, 3.8 rebounds and 2.1 steals and was on the MIAA All-Defensive Team.

McNeill is expected to start in the backcourt with junior college transfer Martin Mitchell, along with the front line of junior Bobby Chipman, sophomore Alex North and 6-9 juco transfer Joe Smith.

This is the seventh straight year the Ichabods have traveled to UNLV, but this will be a new-look team after coach Lon Kruger departed for Oklahoma. The Runnin’ Rebels hired Dave Rice, who was an assistant at Brigham Young the past five years and played on UNLV's 1990 NCAA championship team.

The Rebels, picked second in the Mountain West Conference, return four starters from last year’s NCAA Tournament team led by All-MWC preseason picks Oscar Bellfield and Chace Stanback.

“Coach Rice was on that national championship team, and they’re going to push the pace and be the Runnin’ Rebels again,” Chipman said. “We need to get back on defense and stop them from dunking on us.

“It’s going to be a great test. We always pride ourselves on getting back and getting the defense set, so that will be an extreme test. Even though we don’t match up man-to-man against them, we’ll find out a lot about our defense and who can guard.”

The Ichabods must replace their two dominant offensive threats from last season in center Logan Stutz and forward De’Andre Washington. Chipman is anxious to see how his team handles pressure and finds a way to score.

“The toughest thing when you get into the season is there’s always one or two teams that are really good defensively and will pressure you and play hard,” he said. “We’ll find that in our conference, but we’ll see that early at Vegas and Oklahoma trying to operate against great pressure defense.”
