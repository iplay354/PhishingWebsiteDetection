Welcome to The Nabokovian!

Welcome to the official site of the International Vladimir Nabokov Society (IVNS). You can access most of the site as you wish, but to add to or edit material wiki-style, as we would love you to do, you will have to register.

To subscribe to the announcement list, Nabokv-L, please send an email to this site's general editor, Stephen Blackwell, at his UTK email address.

Please send suggestions, inform us of glitches, or take a stab at editing yourself (if you are a registered user of the site).  

Current Society Highlights

Keith R. Herrmann donates a trove of Vladimir Nabokov's correspondence from the McGraw-Hill, Inc., archives to Houghton Library, Harvard University. To read Mr. Herrmann's introduction to the gift, please go here and here to consult the holdings.

The Society is saddened by news of the passing of Evgeny Belodubrovsky. Tatyana Ponomareva writes:

Our old friend and colleague Evgeny Belodubrovsky  (1941-2025) died [on Oct. 2nd]. He was one of the first Nabokov scholars in Russia and one of those who initiated the opening of the museum in Nabokov's former house in St.Petersburg. An active participant of all Nabokov events for over thirty years, he found and published many archival materials on Nabokov and, recently, authored a book " A Butterly's Fuselage," an encyclopedic collection of his many finds.  

He also wrote a memoir of his childhood in Leningrad entitled "Мы жили на Желябке" ("We lived on Zhelyabka".)
--

Latest Discovery

Trans-oceanic Butterflies

Those who have been interested in Nabokov's (accurate) "Nail of Nome" theory of butterfly dispersal from Eurasia to the New World (his case regarding the Blues), might be interested in new evidence, in different group, for a theory he rejected: butterflies crossing the ocean.

The scientific article (by a team co-led by Naomi Pierce of the MCZ) A trans-oceanic flight of over 4,200 km... is open access. The NYT story about the discovery: Scientists Find First Evidence....

Classics from The Nabokovian

The latest "Classic" is Dr. J.D. Quin's "Nabokov's Cardiology" from 1991.

On retrospective study of Nabokov's novels a high prevalence of cardiac pathology has been noted. A representative series of four patients are described with clinical information derived from the texts. Current management of the medical conditions detailed is discussed along with an explanation for the high frequency of their occurrence.
