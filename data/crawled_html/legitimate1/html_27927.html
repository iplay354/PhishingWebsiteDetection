How Many Websites Use WordPress in December 2025? WordPress Statistics

0 comments

Last updated on December 5, 2025 by Pavel Ciorici

WordPress started as a basic blogging tool in 2003. Now, it serves as the backbone for many websites, ranging from local startups to global enterprises.

WordPress’s growth over the years is impressive. By examining how people use WordPress, we gain insight into web trends, user preferences, and the future of managing online content.

WordPress statistics reveal successful strategies, popular features, and areas needing improvement. We’re covering everything from its market share to its user base, as well as the plugins and themes that contribute to its flexibility.

Let’s examine the specifics, focusing on how many websites use WordPress today.

Quick Facts: WordPress Statistics in December 2025

  • WordPress powers 43.2% of all websites as their CMS.
  • Around 590 million websites are built on WordPress.
  • WordPress dominates the CMS market with a 60.4% share.
  • There are over 30,000 WordPress themes available.
  • More than 70,000 plugins are available for WordPress.
  • WooCommerce is the top e-commerce platform, holding a 33.05% market share.

What Percent of Websites Use WordPress?

As of December 2025, a remarkable 43.2% of all websites globally use WordPress.

W3Techs’ latest data confirms WordPress’s leading position as the go-to content management system (CMS). This statistic isn’t merely about its popularity. It highlights WordPress’s significant impact on digital content creation and management globally.

How Many Websites Use WordPress?

As of December 2025, over 590 million websites use WordPress.

Understanding WordPress’s impact in 2025 requires a thorough examination of website usage statistics.

NetCraft’s report from November 2025 shows a total of 1.36 billion websites. This includes everything from personal blogs and company sites to online stores and social networks.

With 43.2% of all sites built on WordPress, we see its massive footprint. Applying this to NetCraft’s totals, more than 590 million websites globally run on WordPress.

The significance of these numbers is amplified when considering WordPress’s position within the CMS market.

What Is the WordPress CMS Market Share?

When we focus on sites with an identifiable CMS, WordPress’s share jumps to 60.4%. This level of market control goes beyond dominance, nearing a monopoly in the CMS world.

Shopify and Wix, WordPress’s nearest rivals, trail significantly with shares of 6.8% and 5.8%, respectively. This highlights WordPress’s unparalleled lead.

Additionally, it’s interesting to note that 28.5% of the internet functions without a recognized CMS, showing various web development approaches. Yet, among websites choosing a CMS, WordPress’s leadership is clear.

Its consistent innovation and ability to respond to the web’s evolving needs have cemented its top spot in the CMS market.

Source: W3Techs

WordPress Historical Growth Trends

The rise of WordPress to the top of the CMS world is a story of continuous growth and evolution. An analysis from W3Techs outlines WordPress’s expanding influence year after year.

The adoption of WordPress has surged impressively since January 1, 2013, with a 17.4% usage rate across all websites. By December 1, 2025, its market share has climbed to 43.2%.

There’s also been a noticeable decrease in websites operating without a recognized CMS, dropping from 68.2% in January 2013 to 28.5% by December 2025. This indicates a shift towards CMS usage for web development, with WordPress at the forefront of this movement.

How Many WordPress Themes Are There?

As of December 2025, the WordPress.org theme directory contained nearly 14,000 free themes. This figure highlights the dynamic community behind WordPress.

ThemeForest, a top marketplace for WordPress themes, adds another 12,000 paid options to the mix.

Together, these two sources boast over 26,000 themes. Yet, this is just the beginning. Countless free and premium themes are available through various independent theme shops, such as WPZOOM, significantly expanding the selection.

Given the ever-evolving nature of theme creation and distribution, estimating an exact total is tricky. Nonetheless, a cautious guess would put the overall number of WordPress themes well above 30,000.

What Are the Most Popular WordPress Themes?

The world of WordPress themes is vast and varied. A deep dive into data from BuiltWith.com, focusing on the top 1 million websites, showcases the leading themes:

  • Hello Elementor dominates, with 19,229 websites using it. This accounts for an 11.59% share of the top sites.
  • Astra is neck-and-neck with Divi, also on 12,647 sites, representing a 7.62% share.
  • Divi is a close second, appearing on 10,361 sites and securing a 6.24% market share.
  • GeneratePress is chosen by 10,324 sites, making up 6.22% of the top million.
  • Newspaper features on 5,079 sites, 3.06% of the top websites.

On the Envato Market, the most popular themes are:

  • Avada, with 1.046M sales
  • The7, reaching 330K sales
  • BeTheme, close behind with 330K sales
  • Enfold, with 269K sales
  • Flatsome, with 262K sales

These numbers reflect the preferences and trends within the WordPress theme market.

How Much Do WordPress Themes Cost?

The cost of WordPress themes varies, accommodating different budgets and requirements. Plenty of free themes are available for those on a tight budget.

Theme prices vary significantly, ranging from as affordable as $10 to those exceeding $200. The typical price point for a WordPress theme is about $59.

Theme membership subscriptions offer a value proposition for those desiring access to multiple themes through a single purchase. These subscriptions grant entry to a collection of themes. Annual fees for these memberships range from $48 to $399, with the average yearly cost being around $145.

Moreover, some providers offer “lifetime” memberships. This one-time payment model gives members unlimited access to all existing and future themes, eliminating the need for yearly renewals. The average cost for a lifetime membership is approximately $255, making it an appealing option for long-term WordPress enthusiasts and developers seeking extensive theme access for their projects.

Source: CodeinWP

How Many WordPress Plugins Are There?

As of the latest count, there are 59,000 free plugins in the WordPress Plugin Directory and over 5,200 paid plugins on CodeCanyon. Together, these sources account for more than 65,000 plugins.

This number, however, only scratches the surface of what’s available to WordPress users. The WordPress ecosystem is vast, and numerous third-party sites offer a wide range of additional plugins.

Given the dynamic nature of plugin development and distribution, pinning down a precise total is challenging. Nonetheless, it’s safe to say the overall number of WordPress plugins likely exceeds 70,000.

What Are the Most Popular WordPress Plugins?

The WordPress Plugin Directory features several standout plugins, each with over 5 million active installations. These include:

  • Elementor, with 10+ million active installations
  • Yoast SEO, with 10+ million
  • Contact Form 7, with 10+ million
  • Classic Editor, with 9+ million
  • WooCommerce, with 7+ million
  • LiteSpeed Cache, with 7+ million
  • Akismet, with 6+ million
  • WPForms, with 6+ million
  • All-in-One WP Migration, with 5+ million
  • Site Kit by Google, with 5+ million
  • Wordfence Security, with 5+ million

These plugins lead in popularity, showcasing the vast array of functionalities sought after by WordPress users.

How Many Websites Use WooCommerce?

WooCommerce is the leader in the global e-commerce platform market, with a 33.05% share. It is outpacing Shopify, Custom Cart, and Wix, which have shares of 20.85%, 12.49%, and 7.42%, respectively.

By December 2025, WooCommerce’s impact on e-commerce remained profound, driving 12.3% of websites with a recognized CMS, or 8.8% of all websites.

With NetCraft reporting a total of 1.36 billion in November 2025, a detailed look at WooCommerce’s reach is possible.

Calculating the 8.8% rate provides insight into WooCommerce’s extensive use: around 120 million websites are powered by WooCommerce.

These figures underscore WooCommerce’s pivotal role in the online commerce landscape.

Sources: Store Leads, W3Techs

Final Thoughts

The WordPress stats we’ve reviewed unequivocally confirm its supremacy in web development. Commanding over 43.2% of all websites, WordPress cements its status as the top content management system.

The upward trajectory of WordPress’s adoption, combined with its significant market presence, underscores its dependability, ease of use, and extensive support network of themes and plugins.

Looking ahead, WordPress is poised to maintain a prominent role on the web, adapting to and serving the diverse needs of the community.
