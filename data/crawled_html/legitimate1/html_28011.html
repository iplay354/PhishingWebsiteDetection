History of Udhagamandalam

Udhagamandalam has a rich history dating back to the colonial era. The region was originally inhabited by the Toda tribes, an indigenous community with a unique culture. In 1819, the British East India Company, under John Sullivan, discovered Ooty and developed it into a summer retreat. The British influence can still be seen in the colonial-style churches, bungalows, and gardens.

6. Local Cuisine of Ooty

Nilgiri Mountain Railway Or Ooty Mountain Train or Toy Train

Toy train is a beautiful journey in the Nilgiri Mountain. It gives us a memorable experience. The fresh clean air and the breath taking views are not be forgotten easily. The maximum speed of the train is 33km per hour.  The climb to Udgamandalam from the plain used to be a treacherous journey on a ghat road in bullock carts, ponies and palanquins aided by the Nilgiri carrying company's Mail and Express Tonga Service.  British engineers proposed a mountain railway as early as 1854 but it was not until 1891 that Lord Wenlock, the Governor of Madras, laid the foundation of the Nilgiri Mountain Railway.  in 1899, the first train chugged up the slopes from Mettupalayam (36m/1,069ft) to Coonor (1,712/5,617ft), before the rail link was extended to Ooti (2,203m/7,228ft) in 1903.

The Mountain Railway at Darjeeling and Shimla is not so older than the Udhagamandalam, the 46km train journey is the steepest in Asia, with a maximum gradient of 1:12 (8.33 percent). 

The journey starts at Mettupalaam and covers the 46km in 4hours. The train passes through 16 tunnels, forest-clad hills and lush green tea-plantations and rumbles across tall girder bridges.

The railway link was not just an engineering marvel; it also revolutionized the Nilgiri tea industry with swift transportation from the estates to the plains and back to Britain.  Using a rack and pinion Alternate Biting Teeth (ABT) system, it takes nearly 5hr for the ascent with the engine pushing the train from behind.  The scenic terrain past hills, valleys, forests and plantations changes with every mile as the train negotiates 16 tunnels, 27 viaducts and 208 curves.  From Mettupalaiyam station, the train climbs 7km past betelnut, palm and coconut plantations to Kallar, an elevation of 405m/1,329ft. The train crosses Kallar Bridge over the Bhavani River, Adderley Viaduct, Burliar Bridge and 14 tunnels as it crosses tropical jungles via Hill Grove to Conoor (Adderley, Runnymede and Kateri are no longer stops).

Nilgiri Mountain Railway was depicted in David Lean's 1984 film A Passage to India.  Coonoor railway station, a beautiful Colonial structure built in 1897, was one of the scenic locales.  A steam locomotive, x37390, constructed by Swiss Locomotive Works in 1922 and retired after chugging 7.5km, stands outside! From Conor, the train climbs via Wellington, Aruvankadu, Ketti, Lovedale and Fernhill (the highest point).  The train leaves daily from Mettupalayam at 7.10 am returns at 3pm, completing the downhill journey in 3.5hr.  There are also four daily trains each way between Udgamandalam and Coonoor.  In 2005, UNESCO added the Nilgiri Mountain Railway to its list of World Heritage Sites.  Despite having computerized ticketing systems, the NMR still issues manually stamped Edmondson railway tickets, retaining its old-world charm.

The Nilghiri Tahr

The Nilgiri Tahr ungulate native to the Nilgiri Hills and the Southern portion of the Western Ghats range in Tamil Nadu and Kerala states of Southern most India.  It is known locally as the Nilgiri Thar or Simply Ibex. It's closest relatives are sheep. It was placed with the Himalayan Tahr and the Arabian Tahr in the genus Hemitragus.  Ropiquet and Hassanin placed it in a new genus Nilgiritragus. It is genetically more similar to members of the genus ovis than to other Tahrs.  The Nilgiri Tahrs are stocky goats with short, coarse fur and a bristly mane. Males are larger than the females and have a dark color when mature. Both sexes have curved horns, which are larger in the males reaching up to 40 cm for males and 30cm for females.   Adult males weigh 80-100kg and stand about 100cm tall at the shoulder. The tahrs inhabit the open montane grassland habitat of the south Western Ghats montane rain forests eco region.  At elevations from 1200 to 2600m the forests open into grasslands interspersed with pockets of stunted forests known as sholas. These grassland habitats are surrounded by dense forests at the lower elevations. The Nilgiri Tahrs formerly ranged over these grasslands in large herds Hunting and poaching in the nineteenth century reduced their population to as few as 100 animals by the early 20th century.  Since that time their populations have increased somewhat and presently number about 2000 individuals. Their range extends over 400km from north to south and Eravikulam National park is a home to the largest population. The other significant concentration is in the Nilgiri Hills, with smaller populations in the Anamalai Hills Periyar National Park, Palani hills and other pockets in the Western Ghats South of Eravikulam, almost to India’s Southern tip.

List of FAQs about Ooty (Udhagamandalam)

1. What is Ooty famous for?

Ooty, also known as Udhagamandalam, is famous for its breathtaking natural beauty, lush green tea plantations, serene lakes, and colonial-era charm. Located in Tamil Nadu’s Nilgiri Hills, it is known as the “Queen of Hill Stations.” The town’s cool climate, mist-covered mountains, and scenic gardens make it a year-round tourist attraction. Major landmarks like Ootacamund Lake, Botanical Garden, Doddabetta Peak, and Rose Garden add to its charm. Ooty is also known for homemade chocolates, eucalyptus oil, and exquisite tea varieties, which attract thousands of visitors every year.

2. Where is Ooty located?

Ooty is situated in the Nilgiri district of Tamil Nadu, in southern India. It lies at an altitude of approximately 2,240 meters (7,350 feet) above sea level. Surrounded by the Nilgiri mountain range, Ootacamund is part of the Western Ghats, which is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The town is about 89 km from Coimbatore and around 128 km from Mysore, making it easily accessible from both Tamil Nadu and Karnataka.

3. What is the best time to visit Ooty?

The best time to visit Ooty is from March to June and September to November. During these months, the weather remains pleasant, perfect for sightseeing, boating, and trekking. Summer is ideal for exploring tourist attractions, while the post-monsoon season brings fresh greenery and misty landscapes. Winter (December to February) can be quite chilly, making it perfect for honeymooners and those who enjoy cold weather.

4. How can I reach Ooty?

Ooty can be reached by road, rail, and air.

  • By Road: Ooty is well connected by scenic roads from Coimbatore, Mysore, and Bangalore.

  • By Rail: The Nilgiri Mountain Railway, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, connects Mettupalayam to Ooty and offers a beautiful toy train journey.

  • By Air: The nearest airport is Coimbatore International Airport, about 88 km away. From there, taxis and buses are available to reach Ooty.

5. What are the top tourist attractions in Ooty?

Ooty has numerous attractions that captivate every visitor. Some of the must-visit places include:

  • Ooty Lake: Ideal for boating and horse riding.

  • Botanical Garden: Home to thousands of rare plant species.

  • Doddabetta Peak: The highest point in the Nilgiris with panoramic views.

  • Rose Garden: Asia’s largest rose collection.

  • Pykara Falls and Lake: Perfect for picnics and scenic beauty.

  • Cairn Hill, Avalanche Lake, and Emerald Lake: Hidden gems for nature lovers.

  • Tea Museum and Factory: Learn about Ooty’s tea-making tradition.

6. What is the history of Ooty?

Ooty’s history dates back to the early 19th century when it was discovered by the British. Originally inhabited by the Toda tribes, the region was developed as a summer retreat by the British during colonial times. The establishment of tea estates and the Nilgiri Mountain Railway transformed Ooty into a major hill station. The British architectural influence is still evident in its churches, schools, and bungalows.

The Nilgiri Mountain Railway (NMR) is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and a major attraction in Ooty. Established in 1908, it runs from Mettupalayam to Ooty, covering 46 km of scenic landscapes. The slow-moving toy train passes through tunnels, bridges, forests, and tea gardens, offering a mesmerizing journey through the Nilgiris.

History

The Nilgiri Hills were never under dynastic rule. Behind the blue curtain that surrounds the Nilgiris and gives them the name Blue Mountains, they live in peaceful seclusion, while, at their feet, wars are fought between great dynasties.

 However, history records that the Gangars (mid-10th century), Hoysalas (11th century AD), Mysore kings (1610 – 1792 AD) and the ruling families of Kottayam laid claim to the region during their time. In fact, when the British took over this place, they settled the claim of the ruler of Kottayam by giving him a handsome pension. John Sullivan, who was the Collector of Coimbatore, visited the place in 1819, after which he started developing it. In 1823 a route known as the 'bridle path’ was laid which was the best route from the Coimbatore side to the hill country until the first Coonoor Ghat was laid in 1830-1832. Mr Sullivan built the Stone House in 1822, which was to be his home for many years to come. Between 1821 and 1824 many new tracks were laid. He closed the valley near Bishoptown to conduct agricultural and horticultural experiments. In 1826, further impetus was given to the government's plan to use Udagamandalam as a sanatorium and roads were built around the settlement. The settlement soon expanded. Shops were opened by some Bombay Parsis, the Church Missionary Society started a school for Europeans (1832) and William Rumbold began building his house, which later became a hotel (1831). The foothills of Coonoor led to the settlement of Coonoor. Much development took place in the Nilgiris in 1837 during Stephen Rumbold Lushington's governorship, when coffee estates were established here for the first time.

In 1860, the plateau west of Baikara, the Kunthas and the low country north of the plateau west of Baikara, the Kunthas and the low country north of the plateau were annexed to the Coimbatore district.

In 1868, the Nilgiris were separated from Coimbatore and placed under a Commissioner and Assistant who combined revenue, criminal and civil jurisdiction. Further annexations in 1873 and 1877 resulted in the annexation of the Ochterloni Valley and south-eastern Wayanad district. Large areas are cultivated with tea, coffee and cinchona and the district is now the official summer resort of the government. Due to this, it was put on par with other districts and became a Collectorate in 1882.

British officials and citizens in general longed for their own country and English climate, so when Udhagamandalam was discovered, it became a dream come true for the British who longed for something like 'home' in India. Many new buildings came, and the influx of immigrants did not stop. Indian maharajas followed the English aristocracy and palaces vied for a place with British cottages.

Lavish parties became the order of the day; soon social life is a whirlwind of fun and frolic; And Ooty became the queen of hill stations.
