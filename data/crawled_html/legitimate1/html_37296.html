How does Chiropractic work?

Chiropractic care focuses on aligning the spine to improve nervous system function. Through gentle spinal adjustments, chiropractors help relieve pain, improve mobility, and promote overall wellness without drugs or surgery.

Do I need a GP referral?

No, you do not need a referral to book an appointment with a chiropractor. Chiropractors are primary health care professionals, covered by most leading health insurance providers. You can book online or call (08) 7226 6862 to schedule a consultation.

Is Chiropractic safe?

Yes! Chiropractic care is a safe and non-invasive approach to pain relief and spinal health. Our chiropractors use gentle techniques tailored to your needs, ensuring comfort and effectiveness.

Is chiropractic care suitable for children and pregnant women?

Yes! Chiropractic is safe for all ages, including infants, children, and pregnant women. We use gentle, specialised techniques to support spinal health during growth and pregnancy.

Read about Chiropractic care during pregnancy from Pregnancy, Birth & Baby Australia.

How are Chiropractors trained and Regulated?

In Australia, chiropractors undergo extensive training and must meet strict regulatory standards to practice.

  • Chiropractors complete a five-year university degree, which includes clinical training, anatomy, neurology, and radiology.
  • Programs are accredited by Australian universities and meet national healthcare standards.
  • All chiropractors in Australia must be registered with the Australian Health Practitioner Regulation Agency (AHPRA).

What Is the ‘Cracking’ Sound During a Chiropractic Adjustment?

The ‘cracking’ or ‘popping’ sound you hear during a chiropractic adjustment is called cavitation. This occurs when gas bubbles in the synovial fluid of your joints rapidly change pressure, similar to when you crack your knuckles.

Do Chiropractic adjustments hurt?

For most people, chiropractic adjustments are not painful. Many patients feel relief and improved mobility immediately after an adjustment.
