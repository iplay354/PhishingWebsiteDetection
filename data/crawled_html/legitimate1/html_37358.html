Deltarune (2025)
▲
1
▼
Within the game's data is an unused "Talk" button for the Dark World's overworld menu, intended to allow the player to hear observations from Kris' party about the current area, similarly to the tattle feature in the Paper Mario series. The game's code still contains a script, scr_talkroom, which points to this scrapped dialogue, though the only remaining text is a single placeholder line: "Your voice echoes aimlessly." The Talk button also still has an icon and associated code placing it in the center of the menu, although the released game is programmed to omit it from display.

According to producer Chuck Beaver, all of the game's FMV cutscenes were originally planned to be made in 3D using the in-game models. Late in development, Fox requested that they be changed to 2D animation in the style of the TV show, as they felt the 3D-to-2D approach didn't produce results that were "on-model". These 3D cutscenes still appear in the Nintendo DS version, though with some scenes, music, and sound effects removed, likely because they were unfinished.

The level Around the World in 80 Bites was one of the first levels made for the game. According to level designer Jameson Durall, it was known internally as the "Core X" level, which was part of EA's greenlight process to decide whether the development of games would continue. The goal of this level was to prove that the team could deliver everything they had planned, making the game look like and maintain the humor of the original show, and implementing all the core mechanics. The team fully built out this single level so they could use it as a foundation and apply what they learned across the rest of the game.

Metroid Prime 4: Beyond (2025)
▲
2
▼
In an interview with Famitsu, an anonymous Nintendo spokesperson revealed that the inclusion of an open-world area was conceived during the first phase of development under Bandai Namco, based on the fan reception to The Legend of Zelda: Breath of the Wild. The spokesperson explained that both Nintendo and Bandai Namco struggled to balance open-world game design with the Metroid franchise's core focus on unlocking exploration by collecting power-ups, and thus limited it to the hub area of Sol Valley. Complications arose when Nintendo restarted development under Retro Studios (the developer of the previous mainline Metroid Prime games), as Retro lacked the infrastructure to make a new installment from the ground up and had to rely on what had already been completed by Bandai Namco. This meant that they were unable to adapt to the decline in popularity of open-world games and the rise of faster-paced first-person shooter and action titles during the 2020s. Additionally, the team worried that attempting to integrate these changing trends so far into development would disrupt the game's pacing. Consequently, the spokesperson described the game as being "pretty much divorced from the changing of times."

There are two unused areas, called Zone 3 and Zone 4, for the level Enter the Cheatrix that still exist in the game files. According to level designer Ben Johnson, these areas were largely designed as filler, and were cut because they made the level feel too long. Zone 3 mostly repeated gameplay ideas from earlier areas, while Zone 4 was planned to be a combat segment with infinitely spawning enemies, where players would have to fight them while figuring out how to shut down the spawners.

Several level names were originally different during development:

• Lisa the Treehugger was originally called The Treehugger.
• Enter the Cheatrix was originally called Cheater, Cheater, Pumpkin Eater.
• Invasion of the Yokel-Snatchers was originally called The Day Springfield Stood Still.
• Five Characters in Search of an Author was originally called Rhymes with "Complaining".
• Game Over was originally called Meet Thy Player.

Despite the changes, the original names still remain in the game's code and file structure. Many of these early titles appear in early builds, and "Cheater Cheater" and "The Day Springfield Stood Still" were even reused for song names in the game's official soundtrack.

An alternate version of the game's intro can be found in the game files that provides more details on the protagonist Arnold compared to the version seen in the final game. This version of the intro sees Dispatch waking him up after Arnold had finished a 14-hour shift (rather than a 36-hour shift in the final game) in order to assign him to retrieve Fazbear Entertainment's property from Murray's Costume Manor. When Dispatch claims they had informed his friends and family that he would be on vacation, Arnold reveals that his parents were dead, his wife had left him a year ago, and that he hadn't seen his child in a long time.

Deltarune (2025)

2
▼
In a retrospective post detailing the making of Chapters 3 and 4, director Toby Fox revealed that the character design of Tenna was closely inspired by Mr. Tv Dinnah from the game Little King's Story, while the unconventional 3D graphics in the game Ghost Trick: Phantom Detective inspired him to use pre-rendered sprites for Tenna. Fox first attempted to shoot motion capture rigs with a Kinect and then import them into the animation program MikuMikuDance. Later, after drawing concept art for the character in 2017, he attempted to have Kanotynes create a 3D model for him, though this fell through for unspecified reasons. The final model for Tenna's sprites was ultimately put together by PixelatedCrown based on Gigi DG's interpretation of Fox's artwork.

Donkey Kong Country Returns (2010)
subdirectory_arrow_right Donkey Kong Bananza (Game)
▲
1
▼
Donkey Kong Country Returns is notable for introducing entirely new antagonists, the Tiki Tak Tribe led by Tiki Tong, instead of reusing King K. Rool and the Kremlings, who were the series' primary villains since Donkey Kong Country. According to the game's producer Kensuke Tanabe, he and Shigeru Miyamoto discussed early in development whether or not to bring K. Rool back, but they ultimately concluded that it was unnecessary, leading Tanabe to ask Retro Studios to create their own new antagonists. The game's senior designer Kynan Pearson claimed that this was done in an effort to differentiate the game from the previous Rare-developed Donkey Kong Country titles. King K. Rool and the Kremlings would eventually return years later as Spoiler:surprise final antagonists in Donkey Kong Bananza. Pearson felt that the character's return was bittersweet for him due to Nintendo's original mandate when he worked there, while also feeling that it allowed for K. Rool's appearance in the game's climax to be more effective.

subdirectory_arrow_right Sega Mega Drive/Genesis (Platform)
▲
2
▼
The Sega Genesis' YM2612 sound chip is directly based on the Yamaha DX7, an FM digital synthesizer introduced in 1983 which became ubiquitous throughout popular music of the 1980s. These shared elements motivated composer Tom Holkenborg to use his own collection of vintage Yamaha FM synthesizers when writing the soundtrack for the 2020 film Sonic the Hedgehog. According to him, he aimed to translate the "noisy and harsh" sound of the 1991 game into certain parts of the film score with these synthesizers, while complementing more emotional scenes with orchestral pieces.

Gitadora Galaxy Wave Delta (2025)
subdirectory_arrow_right Bemani (Franchise)
▲
1
▼
The GITADORA version of シュレーディンガーの猫 (Schrodinger no neko) is notably credited as "Cait Sith Arranged by Power Of Nature". This marks the first original song/remix by an in-house BEMANI composer to not be preceded by a "BEMANI Sound Team" credit since Pop'n Music Usagi to Neko to Shounen no Yume and Sound Voltex IV: Heavenly Haven introduced the moniker in late 2017. This is due to Konami Arcade Games splitting from Konami Amusement on October 1, 2025 and becoming a new subsidiary headed by Bemani composer Yoshitaka Nishimura. After this split went into effect, Bemani's other in-house composers began making more visible appearances after not doing so since the 2017 rebrand, including the return of the Bemani Namahousou(kari) (BEMANI生放送（仮) ) livestream series for the first time since 2016.
