Yin Can’t Get Rid of Yang, and Yang Can’t Get Rid of Yin

By Amy Hitt

We are all too aware of the scapegoating “they” and “them” being thrown around angrily. There’s plenty to be angry about. There’s a lot of grief to go around underneath that anger. But I believe one of the main problems we have right now is that people on both sides of the aisle truly hope to “get rid of” the other side, and I’m not talking about violent destruction. It’s just that each side hopes to win sufficiently enough to convert or stifle the opposing group forever. But that is impossible because you cannot do away with energy, thank goodness. Yin and Yang are here to stay.

The ancient philosophy of Yin and Yang is about how dualistic natures live together in a moving circle, with half of the circle containing receptive energy, and the other half of the circle containing active energy. The concept of Yin and Yang also states that there is a little active energy in the receptive half and a little receptive energy in the active half. I think another one of our current problems is that each half has squeezed out the “little bit” of the other side within itself that is needed for balance and sanity. And without both halves being in balance, there is no healthy circle of life, hence the consequential illness of the planet.

While we can rightly blame this extremism on social media algorithms and artificial intelligence, the core issue is our willingness to be and stay divided, believing in Yin and Yang’s irreconcilable differences. In this division, we have become divorced from the natural world and our own human nature. Our heads are so far inside the machine that we’ve lost all sense of the sacred. 

Yin and Yang need marriage counseling. They need to be told to stop using words like “always” and “never” when describing the actions of the other. “You ALWAYS do that.” “You NEVER do this.” Yin and Yang need to remember why they got married in the first place. It was love at first sight because opposites attract. They attract until they stop unconditionally accepting one another, differences and all, openly expressing their real emotions, and communicating honestly. 

The aisle they are currently divided by was originally the aisle they marched down together. Back when their life was sweet and beautiful because they were vulnerably transparent about their true selves. Before they became closed off and began defensively pretending to be somebody else.

Neither Yin nor Yang energy will disappear. Energy is a part of nature. Eventually, nature will swallow up us, humans, and then it will be free to start over. Unless we dig deep inside our souls and rediscover our sacred humanity, sans machines, before it’s too late.

Is your organization celebrating the sacred and valuing human nature, or is it simply driving workers to exhaustion in an attempt to exceed production quotas?
